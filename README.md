# Constitutive Coordination: Where Capacities Are Constituted

This essay develops the Constitutive position as a third vertex alongside the Augustinian and Pelagian positions, offered here as a lens for reading a recurring distinction in intervention practice. 
The Augustinian position treats capacity as supplied from outside the system. The Pelagian position treats capacity as already present within the system and released under the right conditions. 
The two positions disagree about where capacity is located, but share the assumption that capacity is located somewhere.

The essay argues that AI-mediated coordination exposes the limits of that assumption. In coupled human and nonhuman systems, some capacities are not held in advance by either party. 
They are constituted through the coupling itself. The Augustinian/Pelagian binary does not give a stable way to name these capacities, 
because its vocabulary assumes a clean separation between inside and outside, supply and release, practitioner and system.

The Constitutive position names the third diagnostic possibility. It does not replace the Augustinian or Pelagian positions, and it is not a synthesis of them. 
It adds a distinct vertex to a trialectic framework in which intervention may be weighted towards supply, release or constitution. 
The aim is to extend the vocabulary available to practitioners working in AI-mediated contexts, so that capacities constituted through coupling can be diagnosed without being misattributed to either side alone.
